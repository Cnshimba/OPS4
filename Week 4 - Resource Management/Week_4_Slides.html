Week 4 Slides: Resource Management with Slurm

Slide 1: The Role of a Resource Manager

  • The Problem: A supercomputer is a shared resource.
    • 100 Researchers.
    • Limited CPUs/RAM.
    • What happens if everyone logs in at once? (Crash/OOM).
  • The Solution: A Resource Manager (Kernel for the Cluster).
    • Accounting: Tracks available resources real-time.
    • Scheduling: Decides WHO runs WHERE and WHEN.

Slide 2: Scheduling Algorithms

  • FIFO (First-In, First-Out):
    • Simple queue.
    • Problem: A small job gets stuck behind a massive job ("Blocking").
  • Backfill Scheduling:
    • The Scheduler "Looks Ahead".
    • If a large job is waiting for resources, the scheduler fills the empty gaps with small jobs that can finish early.
    • Analogy: Tetris (Filling the gaps to maximize score/utilization).
  • Fairshare:
    • Ensures users get what they paid for (50% investment = 50% priority).

Slide 3: Slurm Architecture

  • Slurm: Simple Linux Utility for Resource Management.
    • Standard on Top500 supercomputers.
  • Components:
    • slurmctld (Controller): The Brain (Master Node). Decisions & Queues.
    • slurmd (Daemon): The Worker (Compute Nodes). Executes jobs.
    • slurmdbd (Database): The Memory. History & Usage accounting.
  • Auth: Munge keys ensure trust between all nodes.

Slide 4: Key Commands

  • View State:
    • sinfo: View Partitions and Node States (idle, alloc, drain).
    • squeue: View the Job Queue (Running vs Pending).
  • Submit Work:
    • sbatch script.sh: Submit a non-interactive script.
    • salloc: Request an interactive shell.
  • Control:
    • scancel <jobid>: Kill a job.

Slide 5: The Job Lifecycle

  1. Pending (PD): User submits. Job waits for priority/resources.
  2. Allocated: Scheduler grants resources.
  3. Running (R): slurmd starts the process on the compute node.
  4. Completed (CD): Process exits (success).
  5. Failed (F): Process crashes (non-zero exit code).

Slide 6: Lab Overview

  • Goal: Install Slurm on our Warewulf Cluster.
  • Steps:
    1. Install Munge (Key exchange).
    2. Install slurmctld on Master.
    3. Inject slurmd into the Compute Node Image (since nodes are stateless!).
    4. Reboot & Verify sinfo.
    5. Run "Hello World" on the cluster.
